Predictive Modeling Using Machine Learning¶

Internship Task 2 — Data Science Domain

Goal: Build models to predict an outcome from data, train/test them for accuracy, and visualize performance with confusion matrices and ROC curves.

Problem: Predict whether a Titanic passenger survived or not, from passenger attributes (class, sex, age, fare, family size, port of embarkation).

Algorithms compared: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest.

3. Feature Selection & Preprocessing¶

In [6]:
features = ['pclass', 'sex', 'age', 'sibsp', 'parch', 'fare', 'embarked']
X = df[features].copy()
y = df['survived']

numeric_features = ['age', 'fare', 'sibsp', 'parch']
categorical_features = ['pclass', 'sex', 'embarked']

numeric_pipe = Pipeline([('imputer', SimpleImputer(strategy='median')),
                          ('scaler', StandardScaler())])
categorical_pipe = Pipeline([('imputer', SimpleImputer(strategy='most_frequent')),
                              ('onehot', OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown='ignore'))])

preprocessor = ColumnTransformer([
    ('num', numeric_pipe, numeric_features),
    ('cat', categorical_pipe, categorical_features),
])

models = {
    'Logistic Regression': Pipeline([('prep', preprocessor),
                                     ('clf', LogisticRegression(max_iter=1000, random_state=RANDOM_STATE))]),
    'Decision Tree': Pipeline([('prep', preprocessor),
                               ('clf', DecisionTreeClassifier(max_depth=5, random_state=RANDOM_STATE))]),
    'Random Forest': Pipeline([('prep', preprocessor),
                               ('clf', RandomForestClassifier(n_estimators=200, random_state=RANDOM_STATE))]),
}

for name, model in models.items():
    model.fit(X_train, y_train)

12. Conclusion¶

  • Random Forest performed best on the test set (accuracy 81.56%, F1 0.744), narrowly ahead of Logistic Regression (80.45%, F1 0.724) and the single Decision Tree (77.65%, F1 0.683). Logistic Regression had the highest ROC-AUC (0.843).
  • In 5-fold cross-validation, the three models are close (Decision Tree 81.4%, Random Forest 80.6%, Logistic Regression 79.0%), confirming performance is stable and not a lucky split.
  • Tuning the Random Forest (GridSearchCV) selected max_depth=10, min_samples_split=5, n_estimators=200, with a best CV F1 of about 0.758.
  • Fare, sex, and age are the strongest predictors of survival in the tuned Random Forest, consistent with the historical "women and children first" boarding policy and the fact that passengers who paid higher fares (often first-class) had better access to lifeboats.
  • The model is noticeably better at identifying passengers who did not survive (recall 0.90) than those who did (recall 0.65) — a common pattern when the positive class is the minority.

Limitations: age has about 20% missing values that were median-imputed, which can soften true age-related effects. deck was dropped due to being missing in about 77% of rows.
